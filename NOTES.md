Escale 0.9: Focus Mode hides everything but the page. Type > in the search bar to run any command, or a keyword like “npm react” to search one site.

Escale 0.8.1: sites you were signed into stay signed in after a quit, and with the address above the page its environment shows before the site. Right-click a Space in the rail for its actions.

Escale 0.8: rest the pointer on a bookmark folder to search its sites without opening it. Setup now ends on a summary of your choices, and comes back by itself after you connect GitHub.

Escale 0.7: Escale is now open source, under the GPL, at github.com/kndpt/escale-browser. Clear transparency also lets much more of your desktop show through.

Escale 0.6.1: press Shift–Command–K to find your pull requests and issues, with whether each is open, merged or closed. Private repositories work too: add them in Settings › GitHub.

Escale 0.6 brings GitHub into Bearings, the search bar. Press Shift–Command–K, or Tab after Command–K or Command–T, and the pull requests and issues you have visited or still have open in this Space are there, one line each, with an icon saying whether it is open, draft, merged or closed; Return takes you back to its tab, or reopens it. It works without an account, reading the status from GitHub pages already open; connect a Space in Settings › GitHub, or from the new last step of Welcome, and the lines on screen show their current status, while what you type stays on this Mac.

Escale 0.5 puts the keyboard in your hands: Settings now has a searchable list of every browser command, where you can change a shortcut, see conflicts before replacing one and restore the defaults, and menus show what you chose. Option–Command–Up and Down move between Spaces. A magnifying glass beside New tab searches your open tabs, tabs opened, closed and switched with a shortcut move three times faster (you can turn that off), long tab and bookmark titles in the sidebar keep their beginning and end, and extensions that read text aloud use the current macOS voice. Link routing accepts a pasted address, and a rule can start from a tab's or bookmark's menu.

Escale 0.4 makes moving in easier: it finds Chrome on your Mac and brings over its bookmarks and history in a click, imports local profiles from most other browsers, and can save all your Spaces in one passphrase-protected file to open in Escale on another Mac. Choosing which Space a site's links open in now has its own page in Settings, with a box to try a link, and Settings are drawn smaller to sit in line with the tabs. A page that fails to load keeps its address so Retry tries it again, and the Localhost panel tells you which servers are no longer listening.

Escale 0.3 shows an update coming: a button at the foot of the sidebar says what the new version brings and relaunches with your tabs back as they were. A Google Meet call gets a window of its own, with everyone in it, the shared screen and the call's controls, and it stays open while you work in another Space. Bearings learns which result you pick for what you type, the Network tool lets you step through calls with the arrow keys, pause recording and search inside responses, and a new download flies to its button as a small plane.

Escale 0.2 gives the search bar a name, Bearings, and teaches it to find tabs, bookmarks and history by their words, however a page's address joins them. Environments that share one console address are told apart by the start of their path; a Space's door shows where sound, a video, the camera or the microphone is on; a Google Meet call can float in a small window while you work elsewhere, and changing Space no longer cuts what a page plays. The history panel opens at once with a full history, Clear closes a Space's ordinary tabs in one press, and the camera menu adds Select Area… with a compact capture card. Bookmark folders open and close more smoothly, and the Back and Forward menus read better.

The first Escale: a native Mac browser for the pages around your code, on the WebKit already in macOS. One Space per project, each with its own tabs, bookmarks, history, site data and passwords; named environment links on a bookmark; a Network panel and site-data tools for the page you are on; Chrome extensions on WebKit; imports from Arc, Chrome and other browsers. No account, no telemetry. Built on Search 1.0.1 by Office Commun (MIT).
